# v3 plan: the pre-registered held-out test

We wrote this plan before converting any held-out device, and froze it (with the code, the run configuration and every seed) before any held-out result existed. The results are in [REPORT_V3.md](REPORT_V3.md).

This page is a rewrite, for readability, of the plan we froze. The frozen text is at tag [`v3-held-out-config-r6`](https://github.com/KUQCI/noise-aware-compilation/blob/v3-held-out-config-r6/SPEC_V3.md), and where the two differ, the frozen text is what the test followed. Requirement IDs such as R3-C6 are kept in brackets so they can be matched to the code, the run files and the decision log (P-numbers, at tag `v3.0-results`). All dates are UTC.

## 1. What v3 asks

1. **Layout.** Does the decoder-graph score still choose better placements than the methods people use, on held-out devices of two layouts? One is heavy-hex, where we developed it on CX devices; the other is the square lattice, where we never did. Both use a different native gate, CZ.
2. **Drift.** How much of the gain survives real calibration change, when the decoder also has only the old calibration?
3. **Scale.** Can the scaled score, which replaces the exact score's exponential parts so that it stays tractable as codes grow, rank surface-code placements, and does its choice beat the fixed placement and summed error? At distance 5 it is not yet faster than the exact score (about 1.2 s against 0.06 s per candidate on FakeMiami), so v3 tests its ranking, not whether it is needed. We report the exact score beside it.
4. **Use.** Can someone outside the project see what the package does and run it in a few minutes?

v3 is done when every test in section 13 passes, the report meets section 14, and every v1 and v2 test still passes. No question has a required answer. A correctly measured loss is an acceptable outcome, and the report gives it the same prominence as a win.

## 5. Circuits and noise conversion for CZ devices

**Conversion** [R3-C1]. The converter accepts CZ devices. A CZ gate error *e* becomes 15 Pauli terms of 1.25*e*/15 each, v1's CX rule. IBM lists `cz` in both directions, and we store each direction as its own channel. H follows v1's rule from the published `sx` error *e*<sub>sx</sub>: three Pauli terms of 1.5*e*<sub>sx</sub>/3. `rz` is error-free. Couplers with a two-qubit error above 0.8 are removed, as for CX. A calibration file with no `reset` entry (FakeMiami) gets a reset duration equal to the mean readout length plus the mean `x` length: a measurement followed by a conditional flip. On FakeFez this rule gives the published reset length exactly (1,560 + 24 = 1,584 ns), and on FakeMarrakesh 4% less than published (2,620 against 2,727 ns). The conversion notes record it.

**Repetition codes** [R3-C2]. Circuits A (distance 3 bit-flip), B (distance 3 phase-flip), D5 and D7 (distance 5 and 7 bit-flip), 3 rounds each, are built natively for CZ. Each CX layer of the v2 circuit becomes three layers: H on the CX targets, CZ on the same pairs, H on the targets. Then, until nothing changes, we remove an H on a qubit followed directly by an H on the same qubit in the next layer (no time passes between them), merge two consecutive H-only layers on disjoint qubits, and drop empty layers. An H pair with any other layer between them stays, so the phase-flip code's data qubits idle in the X basis as in v2's circuit B, and B stays a different circuit from A. The bit-flip round becomes H on the ancillas, CZ to each neighbour, H again. The CZ layers keep v2's order and pairs, so a placement uses the same couplers in the same layers. The H layers are their own layers, with idle noise on every waiting qubit.

**Development only** [R3-C3]. We developed and tested the converter and the CZ circuits on FakeFez, FakeMarrakesh and FakeMiami only. A unit test checks that each CZ circuit's noiseless detectors are deterministic and its logical observable is correct.

**Surface codes** [R3-S4]. The rotated surface code, Z memory, distance 5, 5 rounds, native CZ, on the square lattice. The candidates are every SWAP-free placement of the patch (shifts, rotations and reflections that avoid removed couplers), each with every hook-safe stabilizer gate order as a schedule candidate. Hook-safe means the detectors are deterministic and the circuit-level distance under uniform noise is *d*. For Z memory these are: X-type stabilizers row by row (Z-shaped), with Z-type either column by column (N-shaped, Stim's default) or row by row. An N-shaped X-type order has distance 3 at distance 5. A distance-3 surface code (S3, 3 rounds) also runs on FakeBerlin and FakeNighthawk, in no family [R3-S9].

## 6. Methods compared

**The scores under test.** The decoder-graph score for repetition codes; the scaled score at *k* = 640 for surface codes (section 11).

**Main references**, used in the claims:

- The fixed placement.
- Lowest summed error.
- Qiskit's default transpile at level 2. This is what `transpile` and `generate_preset_pass_manager` use when no level is given in Qiskit 2.5.2. It runs VF2Layout, a search that keeps the best layout found within its call limit, not an exhaustive search.
- mapomatic 0.14.0.

**Isolation references**, which ask whether the decoder graph itself helps:

- *Idle-aware summed error* [R3-C11]. For a placement with lowered noisy circuit *C*, the sum over every noise location of *C* on a qubit the placed circuit uses (one that a gate, measurement or reset acts on) of its total error probability. For a Pauli channel that is the sum of its Pauli probabilities (*p*<sub>X</sub> + *p*<sub>Y</sub> + *p*<sub>Z</sub> for one qubit, the sum of the 15 terms for two), whether it follows a gate or models an idle; for a measurement, its readout flip probability; for a reset, its flip probability. It equals summed error plus the total probability of every idle channel on those qubits. Nove's lowering also gives every unused qubit below the highest one used an idle channel, which cannot change an outcome; revision 5's code counted those, so the score tracked the highest qubit index, and revision 6 fixed it (P2-41). It sees everything the simulator sees, as one number, with no decoder graph. Lowest value wins; ties go to the first placement in enumeration order (for the surface code, then the default schedule, then the mapping).
- *The v1 score*: the expected number of detectable faults per shot.

**Exploratory competitors:**

- *Pilot-best* [R3-C12]: among the simulated placements, the one with the fewest failures in the 100,000-shot pilot stage, graded on main-stage counts like every method. It is the "just simulate briefly" competitor. In best-found cases it can only choose within the simulated set, and every such row says so.
- *Detection rate* [R3-C16]: Kan et al. rank placements by how often detectors fire. We use the noise-model form of their proxy, the mean over detectors of (1 − Π<sub>*e*</sub>(1 − 2*p*<sub>*e*</sub>))/2 over the errors *e* that flip the detector, lowest wins. It agrees with sampled detection events (FakeFez D5: 0.0540 computed, 0.0541 from 200,000 shots). It sees the same noise as the decoder-graph score but none of the matching structure.
- Qiskit at levels 1 and 3. Level 1 keeps the trivial layout whenever it needs no SWAP, and on these maps that is the fixed placement.
- Qiskit's level 2 with ten more transpiler seeds, 20261207 to 20261216 [R3-C14]. We record each mapping, its layout pass and any failure, simulate every distinct choice, and report how often the seeds agree.
- v2's VF2Layout baseline with CZ in its target.
- The fast search (section 7).

**How Qiskit and mapomatic see the device** [R3-C9]. They read an IBM-shaped Target with `cz` in place of `cx`, and each qubit's reset at its readout error in the reset = readout setting. v2's VF2Layout baseline gets `cz` at its Nove channel total. mapomatic's cost charges every measurement and every reset at the qubit's readout error whatever the Target says, so at reset 0 it sees reset noise the model does not have; this affects only exploratory results. A Qiskit or mapomatic layout that needed SWAPs, or failed, is recorded and counts as no choice: we never simulate it as that method's choice, and its comparisons leave the family as a missing reference's would. Every Qiskit choice records which pass set its layout (trivial, VF2 or Sabre).

## 7. How placements are chosen

**Exhaustively** [R3-C4]. Every method scores every valid placement, at every distance on every device, including FakeNighthawk distance 7 (12.8 million placements). Each placement is lowered once by `fast_lowering.Lowering`, which gives the same Stim circuit as Nove's own lowering, and the v1 score, the decoder-graph score and summed error are all computed from it. We check this on the development devices (section 13), and every held-out case also checks a seeded sample of 200 placements (or all of them, if fewer) against Nove's lowering. Any difference stops the run and leaves the case without a result. If the timing pilot projects more than 24 hours on patty for one case, that case's decoder-graph choice comes from the fast search instead, and the report says so.

**Failures.** A case that raises an error at any stage writes a failure record in place of its run file, with the stage, the error and the run's provenance. A rerun moves an earlier record to `runs/failed/` and never overwrites it. The main result is written before the exploratory runs start, and those record their own failures. The analysis refuses to run while any planned case has no run file, has a failure record or has unfinished exploratory runs. Only a failed Nove check and a case with no valid placement leave a family, each with its reason.

**Fast search** [R3-C8]. A pre-filter and local search, developed on FakeFez, FakeMarrakesh and FakeMiami against their exhaustive scores and frozen at F3. On held-out devices we report it as exploratory: its choice's score rank and P_L against the exhaustive choice.

## 8. How placements are graded

**Which placements are simulated** [R3-C5]. On the heavy-hex devices, at distance 3, and on FakeBerlin at distance 5 (at most 345,408 placements), every valid placement. FakeBerlin D5's main stage, projected from its own pilot, must fit in 24 wall hours; otherwise it falls back to the best-found set below, and its run file and the report say so. In the other square-lattice cases (FakeBerlin D7, FakeNighthawk D5 and D7), the simulated set is the union of every method's choice, the seed sweep's choices, the top 30 by each of six scores (decoder-graph, v1, summed error, idle-aware summed error, detection rate and mapomatic's cost), and a seeded uniform sample of 1,000 valid placements. We report its cross-fitted best as *best found*, with the uniform sample's size, mean and best interval beside it. There, percentiles and Spearman's ρ are computed over the uniform sample, which the top lists cannot bias, and also over the whole simulated set.

**Shots** [R3-C6]. A pilot stage runs every simulated placement once with 100,000 shots on a separate seed. Each placement's main-stage shots per seed are then the smallest 200,000 × 2^k for which 3 × shots × p̂ ≥ *F*, capped at *C* (a pilot with no failures gets the cap).

- *Family placements* get *F* = 4,000, about a 3% margin of error, and *C* = 102,400,000. They are, on every case of the confirmatory devices and FakeNighthawk, at every setting: the score's choice, and the choices of the fixed placement, summed error, Qiskit's default, mapomatic, the idle-aware summed error and the v1 score. At the directional readout setting, the main setting's family placements are family placements too.
- *Every other placement* gets *F* = 400, about 10%, and *C* = 25,600,000. All of FakeKingston's and FakeAachen's placements are in this tier.

A placement chosen by several methods is simulated once. At 4,000 failures the smallest relative difference the family's strictest Holm step can call significant is about 7%, and about 4% at its loosest, against about 23% and 14% at 400. Only main-stage counts, on three base seeds, enter any failure rate. Pilot counts set shots and choose pilot-best, but never grade.

**Costs** [R3-C7]. We projected every case from development timings before conversion. Revision 6's additions add 1,135 core-hours, for 3,116 in all and 64.9 wall hours on 48 workers (P2-55). The largest case is FakeBerlin's and FakeNighthawk's distance-7 selection with the idle-aware summed error and the detection rate, 8.6 wall hours per setting against the 24-hour limit; FakeBerlin D5's main stage is 5.4 or 6.4 wall hours per setting, at FakeFez's or FakeMiami's P_L, against its 24-hour guard. No case is over a limit. If a heavy-hex device's exhaustive simulation had projected more than 48 hours, that device would have fallen back to the best-found set.

## 9. Statistics and claim rules

**Main family** (`v3-headline`), 52 comparisons at reset = readout:

- 48: the decoder-graph score against the fixed placement, lowest summed error, Qiskit's default and mapomatic, on circuits A, B, D5 and D7, on FakeBoston, FakePittsburgh and FakeBerlin.
- 4: the scaled score against the same four references, on the distance-5 surface code on FakeBerlin.

FakeNighthawk's 20 comparisons of the same kind are reported beside FakeBerlin's with their Newcombe verdicts and unadjusted p-values, labelled synthetic, seen and exploratory. In the family they could decide no claim and would only make Holm stricter on the real-calibration comparisons. FakeKingston's and FakeAachen's 32 comparisons are reported the same way, labelled seen and exploratory.

**Isolation family** (`v3-decoder-graph-isolation`), 25 comparisons at reset = readout, secondary and pre-registered: the decoder-graph score against the idle-aware summed error and the v1 score on the same 12 repetition-code cases (24), and the scaled score against the idle-aware summed error on FakeBerlin's surface code (1). Both rivals see the idle noise that summed error misses, and neither uses a decoder graph, so this family asks whether the decoder graph itself helps. It has its own Holm correction and the same claim rules, worded "the decoder graph helps beyond …" when the row is *beats*. It decides no main claim.

**If FakeBerlin has no SWAP-free distance-5 surface-code placement.** FakeMiami has none: each of its 64 distance-5 placements crosses one of its 7 removed couplers (P2-11). FakeBerlin has 6 couplers at error 1.0, so at least 6 removed couplers, and we had not read which. If none of its 64 placements avoids them, its 4 surface-code comparisons leave the family, the family has 48 members, and every surface-code result is exploratory (FakeNighthawk's and the development checks). v3 then makes no confirmatory surface-code claim, and the report says so. If FakeBerlin has at least one placement, the 4 comparisons stay however few placements there are.

Every other comparison is exploratory and labelled so: FakeKingston, FakeAachen, FakeNighthawk, reset 0, directional readout, Qiskit's other levels and seeds, v2's VF2Layout baseline, the v1 score as a selector, the fast search, pilot-best, the detection rate, the surface code at other *k*, with the exact score, at distance 3 and with its schedule separated, the robustness checks, the all-placement average, drift and the development devices.

**Better and worse after Holm.** A family comparison is *better after Holm* when Holm's procedure rejects it at α = 0.05 (adjusted p at most 0.05) and the estimated difference P_L(choice) − P_L(reference) is below zero, and *worse after Holm* when it is rejected and the difference is above zero.

**Claim rules**, applied per layout and per reference. A claim needs a significant win in more than half of the layout's *n* planned repetition cases, so the threshold is *t* = ⌊*n*/2⌋ + 1. Heavy-hex (FakeBoston and FakePittsburgh) has 8 cases and *t* = 5; the square lattice (FakeBerlin alone) has 4 and *t* = 3. Each wording names the devices and the noise model, for example "on FakeBoston and FakePittsburgh (heavy-hex), under this noise model"; below, *on D* stands for that phrase.

| Wording | Condition |
| --- | --- |
| "beats *X* on D" | Better after Holm in at least *t* cases, and worse in none |
| "mixed against *X* on D: better in *N* cases, worse in *M*" | Better after Holm in at least one case and worse in at least one; every worse case is named |
| "loses to *X* on D" | Worse after Holm in at least one case and better in none; every worse case is named |
| "better after Holm in *N* of *n* cases (fewer than *t* = *T*); no layout claim against *X* on D" | Anything else: better in fewer than *t* cases, and worse in none |

On heavy-hex, every claim also gives each device's better and worse counts, so a reader can see when one device carries it. For FakeBerlin's surface code, per reference: "beats" when better after Holm, "loses to" when worse, otherwise "no difference shown", qualified by the headroom check (section 14). A case with no valid placement for a method or reference leaves the family, which shrinks accordingly, and the report names it; *t* stays 5 or 3. The claims are about these snapshots under this noise model. They do not extend to the real chips, to other days' calibrations or to other devices of the same family.

**Secondary endpoints**, pre-registered but in neither family: each method's cross-fitted relative regret on every held-out case; Spearman's ρ between each score and simulated P_L; the mean P_L over all placements where every placement is simulated, and each method's percentile among the simulated placements (and among the uniform sample in best-found cases), beside every fixed-placement figure; every P_L also per round, 1 − (1 − P_L)<sup>1/rounds</sup>; and the drift metrics of section 10.

**Robustness checks** (all exploratory; the report lists every comparison whose result after Holm changes under each):

- *Correlated matching and d rounds* [R3-C10]. On every confirmatory case at reset = readout, each family placement's main-stage samples are decoded again with PyMatching's correlated matching (`enable_correlations=True`, PyMatching 2.4.0), built from the same approximate detector model. The re-decode reuses the main stage's chunk seeds and must reproduce its uncorrelated count on every chunk, so both decoders see the same syndromes. The family placements of D5 and D7 are also simulated for *d* rounds (5 and 7), with their own pilot and seeds and the family's shots rule.
- *Readout by direction* [R3-C13]. Nove's readout error is symmetric, and the converter stores each qubit's published `readout_error`, the mean of its two directions. A Z-basis repetition code reads 0 on every measurement when nothing has gone wrong, so the error it meets is P(1|0), the published `prob_meas1_prep0`, about half of `readout_error` on FakeFez (median ratio 0.52). This setting gives each qubit's readout and reset flip probability its P(1|0), capped at 0.5, and gives Qiskit's and mapomatic's Target the same values. It runs on A, D5 and D7 of the confirmatory devices: every method chooses again, and every choice at this setting and at the main setting is simulated under it. The main family is evaluated with this setting's own choices and, separately, with the main setting's choices.
- *Grading variants* [R3-C15]. Each family placement of the confirmatory devices is simulated again under a model that changes one thing, and decoded with the original model's weights, as a decoder with only the published calibration would be. The variants are: every CZ at the device's shortest and at its longest published CZ length; each qubit's idle Z rate doubled (IBM's T2 is an echo measurement); each CZ error minus its two qubits' published `sx` errors; independent log-normal factors exp(δ*z*) on every channel at δ = 0.1 and 0.25, four seeded draws each; and, on Z-basis repetition codes, readout and reset at P(1|0). Shots follow the non-family rule.
- *Placement versus schedule* [R3-C17]. The scaled score chooses a placement and a schedule together, while every surface-code reference uses the default schedule, so a difference could come from the schedule. Two rows separate them without new simulation: the scaled score held to the default schedule against every reference, and each reference's placement under the scaled choice's schedule against the scaled choice.

**Model mismatch.** Every family comparison grades a placement under the noise model that chose it. We considered a run that selects under the converted model and grades under a perturbed one with stale decoder weights, and did not add it in revision 5: v2's synthetic drift re-graded with stale weights (section 10) is that design at three noise strengths, and the real-drift runs do it with real calibration change (P2-35). Revision 6 added the grading variants above. The family still grades under the model that chose; the remaining gap is hardware, which is out of scope.

## 10. Real calibration drift

- **Snapshots** [R3-D1]. Dated calibration files come from older `qiskit-ibm-runtime` releases, each read from that release's package as a zip archive and never installed or imported (P2-21), with each file's hash and source release recorded. Pairs: FakeHanoi 2025-02-12 → 2025-02-26 (14 days) and 2024-05-27 → 2025-02-26 (9 months); FakeFez 2025-01-23 → 2025-02-26 (1 month); FakeTorino 2024-02-28 → 2025-02-26 (12 months).
- **Grading** [R3-D2]. Every method chooses on the old snapshot and is graded by simulation under the new one. The decoder's weights come from the old snapshot, because a real decoder only has the old calibration. A second grading with new-snapshot weights is reported as context.
- **Circuits** [R3-D3]. A, B, D5 and D7 on each device (CX on FakeHanoi, CZ on FakeFez and FakeTorino), at both reset settings, reset = readout first.
- **Metrics** [R3-D4]. The absolute gain kept, P_L(fixed) − P_L(old choice), both under the new snapshot, with a 95% interval; the gain a fresh choice would have had; and their ratio, only where the fresh gain's interval has a lower end at least half its estimate (P1-4).
- **Synthetic drift re-graded** [R3-D5]. v2's synthetic drift is re-graded with stale decoder weights too, so synthetic and real drift are comparable. This is the only rerun of v2 data, and it writes new files.
- **A robust rule** [R3-D6], secondary and pre-registered. Choose by the mean score over FakeHanoi's two older snapshots (2024-05-27 and 2025-02-12), graded on 2025-02-26, against choosing on the latest older snapshot (2025-02-12).
- **Couplers that became uncalibrated** [R3-D7]. A coupler that is valid in the older snapshot and has error 1.0 in the newer one is graded at the converter's limit, 0.8 (P2-22), but its true error is unknown. Every drift row that uses such a coupler is graded again with it at its older value and nothing else changed, so both ends are reported. Six rows use one, all the decoder-graph score's old choice on FakeTorino over 12 months: it keeps +31% to +81% of the fixed placement's P_L at the older values, against −153% to −799% at the limit.

## 11. The scaled score

- **Method** [R3-S1]. The scaled score replaces the exact score's two exponential parts. The distribution of a chain's flipped weight is a dynamic program over edge weights log((1−p)/p) rounded to a grid, and the chains are the top *k* by weight, found with Dijkstra on the doubled graph (detector, observables flipped so far) followed by a k-shortest-paths search. It uses no sampling.
- **Grid step and repetition-code *k*** [R3-S2]. Chosen on development data only. The grid step is the largest of 0.5, 0.2, 0.1, 0.05, 0.02 and 0.01 for which, with the chains fixed to the exact score's own minimum-length cycles, the scaled score is within 1% of the exact score on every candidate of the 10 v2 cases. It is chosen first, then *k* at that step: the smallest *k* (from 1, 2, 5, 10, 20, 50, 100, 200, 500, 1000) for which, on all 10 v2 cases, the scaled score's top choice equals the exact score's and Spearman's ρ between them over all candidates is at least 0.99.
- **Surface-code *k*** [R3-S2, revision 4]. Repetition codes have few near-lightest chains and the surface code has many, so the repetition-code *k* can be far too small. We use the scaled score only on the surface code, with *k* chosen by convergence: the smallest of 10, 20, 40, 80, 160, 320 and 640 for which, on each of four FakeMiami cases (distance 3 as converted and distance 5 with its removed couplers restored, each at both reset settings), the score at 2*k* ranks the candidates with ρ ≥ 0.99 against the score at *k* and picks the same top choice. If none does, the *k* with the largest minimum ρ is used, ties to the smaller. The grid step stays 0.05.
- **What happened** (P2-27). The criterion failed: the distance-5 ranking at reset = readout never reached ρ ≥ 0.99 between *k* and 2*k* (at reset 0 it reached 0.992 at *k* = 320, where the top choice changes, and 0.991 at 640), and the fallback gave *k* = 80. After seeing that, we chose *k* = 640, the value from the list with the best worst-case ρ against the simulated P_L of the tuning set (0.937 at distance 5 and 0.962 at distance 3, against 0.836 and 0.984 at *k* = 80). The choice uses development data only, and the report states it as a deviation from the rule. The ranking rests on 128 FakeMiami candidates that are 16 qubit sets with 8 placements each, so it has far fewer independent points than candidates (P2-34). As sensitivity choices, every surface-code candidate is also scored at *k* = 10 (the repetition-code value) and *k* = 80 (the rule's fallback), and their choices are reported as exploratory.
- **The tuning set** [R3-S3]. We checked the scaled score against simulated P_L on FakeMiami surface codes (distance 3 and 5) before the freeze, reported the result and applied no threshold. Because we chose *k* on it, it is a tuning set, not a validation. FakeMiami has no SWAP-free distance-5 placement (each of the 64 crosses one of its 7 removed couplers), so its distance-5 check restores those couplers at the median CZ channel of the kept ones, labelled synthetic. The out-of-sample tests are FakeBerlin, whose summary figures we saw only after choosing *k* = 640, and FakeNighthawk, whose CZ values we read on Nov 9, before that choice. So FakeBerlin is the one clean test.
- **Surface-code baselines** [R3-S5]. The fixed placement with the default schedule; summed error (blind to the schedule, so it takes the default); Qiskit's default at level 2 (main) and levels 1 and 3 (exploratory); and mapomatic (layout only, default schedule).
- **Speed** [R3-S6]. Target: the whole distance-5 selection on one patty core in at most 60 seconds at the frozen *k*, with the exact score's time on the same candidates reported beside it. At *k* = 640 we expected to miss it (about 1.2 s per candidate, 128 candidates on FakeMiami), and the exact score is faster at distance 5 (about 0.06 s per candidate), so v3 shows only whether the scaled score ranks well, not that it is needed.
- **A ranking, not an error rate** [R3-S7]. The CLI output, the API docstrings and the report present the score as a ranking signal. We fit no correction factor.
- **Dropped from the roadmap** [R3-S8]. Mixed repetition-code schedules (a two-layer schedule must be uniform, so mixing needs a third layer) and the correction factor.
- **Which result leads** [R3-S9]. The scaled score at *k* = 640 is the surface code's main method. The exact decoder-graph score is computed on every candidate too, and reported beside it as exploratory, so a reader can see whether the approximation changed the choice.

## 14. What the report must contain

The report follows the roadmap's rules: a one-screen summary with a claims table and a forest plot; one question per section, answered first; each number once; the verdicts *better*, *worse* and *tie*; a main body of about 4,000 words and 8 figures and tables at most; limitations and deviations in the main body; and losses and ties as prominent as wins. In addition:

- Every table and figure says whether it shows development or held-out devices, and which reset setting [R3-R1].
- Every FakeNighthawk number is labelled synthetic, seen and exploratory, and every FakeBerlin claim carries its note [R3-R2].
- The reset study's result is in the main body, worded as measured [R3-R3]. At reset = readout the v1 score lost to the fixed placement on v1's seeds in three FakeHanoi distance-3 circuits (3 rounds, 5 rounds and X basis; the 5-round case had been v1's −15.2% gain at reset 0). On fresh seeds the loss replicates by its Newcombe interval in one of the three (3 rounds, +10.5%), and the other two are ties. The decoder-graph score is better than the fixed placement by its Newcombe interval in the 3- and 5-round circuits at both settings, though the 3-round result at reset = readout does not hold after Holm (adjusted p = 0.41), and its reset-0 figure is v2's committed run.
- Best-found results give the size of the simulated set and the uniform sample's best interval, so a reader can judge how far the true best could be [R3-R4].
- If FakeBerlin has no distance-5 surface-code placement, the report says the family had 48 members, the claims table has no surface-code row, and the scale question is answered by exploratory results only [R3-R5].
- **The surface code's room to differ** [R3-R6]. Two checks on each square-lattice device's own results, neither of which changes a verdict. *Headroom:* the fixed placement's cross-fitted regret on the distance-5 surface code; when it is a tie, a "no difference shown" claim adds that the best placement found was not better than the fixed one either, so the device left no room to show a difference. *Suppression:* Λ, the best per-round P_L at distance 3 over the best at distance 5; above 1, more distance helps under this model. Revision 5 had pre-written "no difference shown" because FakeMiami sits near threshold, but its per-round figures were not computed by section 9's formula (by it, FakeMiami's best is 1.41% per round at distance 3 and 1.43% at distance 5, P_L 0.0418 over 3 rounds and 0.0695 over 5), and two distances on one development device cannot explain why a held-out comparison ties.
- **The fixed placement in context** [R3-R7]. On development devices the fixed placement sits at or below the 16th percentile of P_L in 20 of 28 runs (22 of 28 at the 17th), so it is usually a favourable draw, though not always: FakeJakarta A puts it at the 87.5th percentile and FakeHanoi D5 at about the 41st and 37th (two runs). Every fixed-placement figure has the all-placement mean P_L and the fixed placement's and the choice's percentiles beside it.
- Results of the 3-round repetition codes are called "3-round" wherever the number of rounds is not in the sentence, with the *d*-round results beside them [R3-R8].
- Beside each claim, the report lists every comparison whose result after Holm changes under a robustness check, directional readout or another Qiskit seed, and every case that failed, left a family or fell back to a best-found set, with its reason [R3-R9].

## 15. Known limits, scope and rules

**Known limits**, which the report discloses (v1's and v2's still apply):

- **Resets** [K12]. Reset = readout treats a reset failure as a readout error followed by a wrong flip. Real resets may fail differently, through leakage or measurement-induced transitions.
- **Synthetic calibration** [K13]. FakeNighthawk's calibration is synthetic.
- **Snapshot noise** [K14]. Real drift is measured between dated snapshots, each of which carries its own single-calibration noise.
- **Best-found regret** [K15]. In FakeBerlin D7 and FakeNighthawk D5 and D7 (and FakeBerlin D5 if its guard trips), regret is a lower bound. At distance 7 this cannot be fixed within v3: simulating all 12.8 million placements at the projected 15.6 core-seconds each would take about 56,000 core-hours per setting, against v3's 3,116 in all.
- **mapomatic and resets** [K16]. mapomatic's cost charges every reset at readout error, so at reset 0 it ranks layouts by reset noise the model does not have (exploratory results only).
- **One snapshot per device** [K17]. p-values cover Monte Carlo noise only, not calibration-to-calibration variation, so the claims are about these snapshots. We checked all 84 release tags of `qiskit-ibm-runtime` (file hashes only, no contents): FakeBoston, FakePittsburgh, FakeBerlin and FakeMiami each have exactly one calibration file, unchanged from 0.47.0 to 0.52.0.
- **Graded under the model that chose** [K18]. Grading under a different, real noise process needs hardware runs, which are out of scope; the drift runs and grading variants are the closest simulated answers.
- **Single-qubit error counted twice, and no crosstalk** [K19]. IBM's two-qubit error comes from benchmarks whose layers include single-qubit gates, and our circuits charge their H gates again, so single-qubit error around each CZ is partly counted twice. The `cz-without-1q` grading variant tests the other end of that range. IBM also measures the two-qubit error without neighbouring gates running, so crosstalk is missing, and we cannot add it without measurements the fake backends do not ship.
- **Pinned Qiskit** [K20]. Qiskit is pinned at 2.5.2 by the lock file (2.6.2 is current), and a later release may lay out differently. Changing it would break the lock and the code freeze every run file is checked against; the seed sweep shows how much the choice moves with the transpiler seed alone.
- **Approximate detector model** [K21]. v1 said no exact detector model exists, because Nove's own detector model refuses calibrated two-qubit channels. One does exist for this noise: each CZ channel is 15 equal Pauli terms, which is exactly DEPOLARIZE2, and each single-qubit Pauli channel factors exactly into independent X, Y and Z flips. Decoding with it instead of Stim's approximate model changes nothing measurable: on 15 development placements (FakeFez D5 and D7, FakeMiami D5), 1,000,000 shots each, the two decoders disagree on 18 of 15 million shots, with 21,041 failures against 21,043, while mechanism probabilities differ by at most 4.4% (median 0.14%).

**Out of scope:** hardware runs (they need an IBM Quantum account), ECR devices, surface codes on heavy-hex devices, distance-7 surface codes (they need a 13×13 region, and the square lattice is 10×12), routing and SWAP insertion, and changes to Nove.

**Rules we bound ourselves to** (v1's X1 to X10 and v2's X11 to X15 still apply):

- **X16.** No converting, scoring or simulating a held-out device before F4, and no reading any of its calibration values beyond what section 2 lists. This binds every reader or helper briefed on the project, and every brief states it.
- **X17.** No changing the family, the claim rules, the shots rule, the seeds or any file `freeze_f4.json` records after F4, unless the record lists the change with its reason.
- **X18.** No changing either score or the fast search after F3, except the surface-code *k*, which revision 4 reopened and the second F3 commit records, before any held-out conversion.
- **X19.** No mixing machines within a held-out device's results.
- **X20.** On patty, nothing outside the home directory, nothing above `nice -n 19`, and no more than 48 threads.
