## 2. Goal and definition of done

v3 asks four questions:

1. **Layout:** does the decoder-graph score still choose better placements than the methods people use, on held-out devices of two chip layouts, heavy-hex (where it was developed, on CX devices) and the square lattice (where it never was), with a different native gate (CZ)? (Part A)
2. **Drift:** how much of its gain survives real calibration change, when the decoder also only has the old calibration? (Part B)
3. **Scale:** can the scaled score, which replaces the exact score's exponential parts so that it stays tractable as codes grow, rank surface-code placements, and does its choice beat the naive placement and summed error in simulation? At d=5 it is not yet faster than the exact score (about 1.2 s against 0.06 s per candidate on FakeMiami, R3-S6), so v3 tests its ranking, not a need for it; the exact score is reported beside it (R3-S9). (Part C)
4. **Use:** can someone outside the project see what it does and run it in a few minutes? (Part D)

v3 is done when every test in section 9 passes, the report meets section 10, and every v1 and v2 test still passes. No question has a required answer: a correctly measured loss is an acceptable outcome, and the report gives it the same prominence as a win.

## 4. Part A: new layouts and CZ (roadmap Step 2)

| ID | Requirement |
| --- | --- |
| R3-C1 | The converter accepts CZ devices. A CZ gate error *e* becomes 15 Pauli terms of 1.25 *e* / 15 each, the v1 CX rule; IBM lists `cz` in both directions and each direction is stored as its own channel. H follows v1's rule from the published `sx` error *e*<sub>sx</sub> (three Pauli terms of 1.5 *e*<sub>sx</sub> / 3); `rz` is error-free. Couplers whose two-qubit error exceeds 0.8 are removed, as for CX. A props file with no `reset` entry (FakeMiami) gets a reset duration of the mean readout length plus the mean `x` length, a measurement followed by a conditional flip; on FakeFez this rule gives its published reset length exactly (1,560 + 24 = 1,584 ns) and on FakeMarrakesh 4% less than its published one (2,620 against 2,727 ns), and the conversion notes record it |
| R3-C2 | The repetition codes A (d=3 bit-flip), B (d=3 phase-flip), D5 and D7 (bit-flip), 3 rounds each, are built natively for CZ devices: each CX layer of the v2 circuit becomes three layers, H on the CX targets, CZ on the same pairs, H on the targets. Then, until nothing changes: an H on a qubit and an H on the same qubit in the next layer are both removed (no time passes between them); two consecutive H-only layers on disjoint qubits are merged; empty layers are dropped. An H pair with any other layer between them stays, so the phase-flip code's data idle in the X basis as in v2's circuit B, and B stays a different circuit from A. The bit-flip round becomes H on the ancillas, CZ to each neighbour, H again. The CZ layers keep v2's order and pairs, so a placement uses the same couplers in the same layers; the H layers are their own layers, with idle noise on every waiting qubit |
| R3-C3 | The converter and the CZ circuits are developed and tested on FakeFez, FakeMarrakesh and FakeMiami only. A unit test checks the CZ circuit's noiseless detectors are deterministic and its logical observable is correct |
| R3-C4 | **Selection is exhaustive.** Every method scores every valid placement, at every distance on every device, including FakeNighthawk d=7 (12.8M placements). Each placement is lowered once, by `fast_lowering.Lowering`, which gives the same Stim circuit as Nove's lowering (checked on the development devices as T3-C6 says; every held-out case also checks a seeded sample of 200 placements, or every placement when it has fewer, against Nove's own lowering, and any difference stops the run, leaves the case without a result and is reported), and the v1 score, the decoder-graph score and summed error are computed from it; summed error and mapomatic's layout costs run on every worker. If the timing pilot (R3-C7) projects more than 24 hours on patty for one case, that case's decoder-graph choice comes from the local search of R3-C8 instead, and the report says so. **Failure records (revision 6).** A case that raises at any stage writes a failure record in place of its run file, with the stage, the error and the run's provenance; a re-run moves an earlier record to `runs/failed/` and never overwrites it; the main result is written before the exploratory runs start, which then record their own failures. The analysis refuses to run while a planned case has no run file, has a failure record or has unfinished exploratory runs. Only a failed Nove check (above) and a case with no valid placement (section 7) leave a family, each with its reason |
| R3-C5 | **Evaluation (revision 6).** On the heavy-hex devices, at d=3, and on FakeBerlin at d=5 (345,408 placements at most), every valid placement is simulated. FakeBerlin D5's main stage, projected from the case's own pilot stage in proportion to the shots (an upper bound), must fit in 24 wall hours at its setting; otherwise that case falls back to the best-found set below, and its run file and the report say so. In the other square-lattice cases (FakeBerlin D7, FakeNighthawk D5 and D7) the simulated set is the union of: every method's choice; the choices of R3-C14's seed sweep; the top 30 by each of the decoder-graph score, the v1 score, summed error, the idle-aware summed error, the detection rate and mapomatic's cost; and a uniform random sample of 1,000 valid placements (seeded). Its cross-fitted best is reported as *best found*, with the random sample's size, mean and best-found interval beside it. There, percentiles and Spearman's ρ are computed over the uniform sample, which the top lists cannot bias, and also over the whole simulated set. Revision 5 had the top 30 by three scores, 100 uniform placements and percentiles over the simulated set only |
| R3-C6 | **Shots.** A pilot stage runs every simulated placement once with 100,000 shots on a separate pilot seed. Each placement's main-stage shots per seed are then the smallest 200,000 × 2^k with 3 × shots × p̂ ≥ *F*, capped at *C*; p̂ = 0 gives the cap. A *family placement* gets *F* = 4,000 (about a 3% relative half-width) and *C* = 102,400,000; every other placement gets *F* = 400 (about 10%) and *C* = 25,600,000. The family placements are, on every case of the confirmatory devices and of FakeNighthawk, at every setting: the headline score's choice; the choices of the naive placement, summed error, Qiskit's default at level 2 and mapomatic; and (revision 6) the isolation references' choices, the idle-aware summed error and the v1 score (section 7). A placement chosen by several methods is simulated once. At 4,000 failures the smallest relative difference the family's strictest Holm step can call significant is about 7%, and about 4% at its loosest, against about 23% and 14% at 400. Only main-stage counts, on three base seeds, enter any failure rate; pilot counts set shots and may select (R3-C12) but never grade |

| R3-C11 | **Idle-aware summed error (revision 5).** For a placement *m* with lowered noisy circuit *C*(*m*) (the circuit selection and simulation use), S<sub>idle</sub>(*m*) = Σ over every noise location ℓ of *C*(*m*) on a qubit the placed circuit uses (one that a gate, measurement or reset acts on; revision 6) of its total error probability *P*<sub>ℓ</sub>: for a Pauli channel, the sum of its Pauli probabilities (*p*<sub>X</sub> + *p*<sub>Y</sub> + *p*<sub>Z</sub> for a single-qubit channel, the sum of the 15 terms for a two-qubit one), whether it follows a gate or models an idle; for a measurement, its readout flip probability; for a reset, its flip probability. It equals summed error plus the total probability of every idle channel on those qubits (`baselines.idle_aware_summed_error`, tested on FakeFez and FakeMiami). Nove's lowering also gives every unused physical qubit below the highest one used an idle channel (v1's K5, P2-17), which cannot change an outcome; revision 5's code counted them, so S<sub>idle</sub> tracked the highest qubit index (P2-41). It is scored on every placement and every surface-code candidate |
| R3-C12 | **Pilot-best (revision 5, exploratory).** Among the simulated placements of a case, the one with the fewest pilot failures (pilot shots are equal; ties to the first placement in enumeration order). The pilot runs on its own seed, so pilot-best is graded on main-stage counts like every method, against the headline score and every reference. It is the "just simulate briefly" competitor, at the pilot's 100,000 shots; in R3-C5's best-found cases it chooses only within the simulated set, and every such row says so |
| R3-C13 | **Direction-aware readout (revision 6, exploratory).** Nove's readout error is symmetric (K3), and the converter stores each qubit's published `readout_error`, the mean of its two directions. A Z-basis repetition code reads 0 on every measurement when nothing has gone wrong, so the direction it meets is P(1\|0), the published `prob_meas1_prep0`, about half of `readout_error` on FakeFez (median ratio 0.52). The *directional* setting sets each qubit's readout flip probability, and its reset flip probability (reset = readout), to its P(1\|0), read from the device's props file (checked against `data/devices/SHA256SUMS`) and capped at 0.5, and gives Qiskit's and mapomatic's Target the same values (`nac_bench.directional`). It runs on A, D5 and D7 of the confirmatory devices, after the headline setting: every method selects again, and every choice of the case at this setting and at the headline setting is simulated under it, the family placements of both at the family's shots. The headline family is evaluated at this setting with its own choices and, separately, with the headline setting's choices; both are exploratory |
| R3-C14 | **Qiskit's transpiler seed (revision 6, exploratory).** Qiskit's default at level 2 also runs with ten more transpiler seeds (20261207 to 20261216) on every case; each mapping, its layout pass and any failure are recorded, every distinct choice is simulated, and the report gives how often the seeds agree and how each distinct choice compares with the headline score |
| R3-C15 | **Grading variants (revision 6, exploratory).** Each family placement of the confirmatory devices at the headline setting is simulated again under variants of the model that keep the placement and change one thing the converted model fixes, and is decoded with the selecting model's weights, as a decoder with only the published calibration would be (`nac_bench.grading_variants`): every CZ at the device's shortest and at its longest published CZ length (K2); each qubit's idle Z rate doubled (IBM's T2 is an echo measurement); each CZ error less its two qubits' published `sx` errors (K19); independent log-normal factors exp(δ*z*) on every channel at δ = 0.1 and 0.25, four seeded draws each, which carries R3-D5 to the CZ devices; and, on the Z-basis repetition codes, readout and reset at P(1\|0) (R3-C13). Shots follow the other placements' rule from the placement's own pilot count. Both families are recomputed under each variant, and every comparison whose result after Holm changes is listed |
| R3-C16 | **Detection rate (revision 6, exploratory).** Kan et al. rank placements by how often detectors fire. The noise-model form of their proxy, the mean over detectors of (1 − Π<sub>*e*</sub>(1 − 2*p*<sub>*e*</sub>))/2 over the errors *e* of the placement's undecomposed approximate DEM that flip the detector, is scored on every placement and surface-code candidate (lowest wins; ties as for S<sub>idle</sub>), and its choice is compared with the headline score and every reference. It agrees with sampled detection events (FakeFez D5: 0.0540 computed, 0.0541 from 200,000 shots; `tests/test_v3_detection_rate.py`). It sees the same noise as the decoder-graph score but none of the matching structure, so it asks whether the decoder graph adds anything beyond "fewer detection events" |
| R3-C17 | **Placement against schedule (revision 6, exploratory).** The scaled score chooses a placement and a schedule together, while every surface-code reference takes the default schedule, so a surface-code difference can come from the schedule rather than the placement. Two exploratory rows separate them, with no extra simulation because every candidate is simulated: the scaled score held to the default schedule (`scaled-default-schedule`) against every reference, and each default-schedule reference's embedding under the scaled choice's schedule (`<reference>@scaled-schedule`) against the scaled choice |

## 7. The headline family and the claim rules

**Family `v3-headline`, 52 comparisons, at reset = readout:**

- 48: the v2 decoder-graph score against the naive placement, lowest summed error, Qiskit's default at level 2 and mapomatic, on circuits A, B, D5 and D7 on FakeBoston, FakePittsburgh and FakeBerlin (revision 5; revision 4 named FakeKingston and FakeAachen).
- 4: the scaled score against the same four references, on the d=5 surface code on FakeBerlin.

FakeNighthawk runs every case, and its 20 comparisons of the same kind are reported beside FakeBerlin's with their Newcombe verdicts and unadjusted p-values, labelled synthetic, seen and exploratory. In the family they could decide no claim and would only make Holm stricter on the real-calibration comparisons (revision 4). FakeKingston and FakeAachen run every case too, and their 32 comparisons are reported the same way, labelled seen and exploratory (revision 5).

**Family `v3-decoder-graph-isolation`, 25 comparisons, at reset = readout (revision 5, secondary, pre-registered).** On the same 13 cases: the decoder-graph score against the idle-aware summed error (R3-C11) and against the v1 score, on circuits A, B, D5 and D7 of FakeBoston, FakePittsburgh and FakeBerlin (24), and the scaled score against the idle-aware summed error on FakeBerlin's surface code (1). Both rivals see the idle noise the summed-error baselines miss, and neither uses a decoder graph, so this family answers whether the decoder graph itself helps. It has its own Holm correction and the same claim rules and wording, reported as "the decoder graph helps beyond …" when the row is *beats*. From revision 6 its choices are family placements, with the family's shots, and the sensitivity runs and grading variants cover them. It decides no headline claim.

**If FakeBerlin has no SWAP-free d=5 surface-code embedding.** FakeMiami has none: each of its 64 d=5 embeddings crosses one of its 7 removed couplers (P2-11). FakeBerlin has 6 couplers at error 1.0, so at least 6 removed couplers, and which ones is not read before F4 (section 1). If none of its 64 embeddings avoids them, its 4 surface-code comparisons leave the family, the family has 48 members, and every surface-code result in v3 is exploratory: FakeNighthawk's and the development checks of R3-S2 and R3-S3. v3 then makes no confirmatory surface-code claim, and the report says so (R3-R5). If FakeBerlin has at least one embedding, the 4 comparisons stay in the family however few embeddings there are.

Every other comparison (FakeKingston, FakeAachen, FakeNighthawk, reset 0, the directional setting, Qiskit's default at levels 1 and 3 and at other seeds, v2's VF2Layout baseline, the v1 score as a selector, the fast search, pilot-best, the detection rate, the surface code at *k* = 10 and 80, with the exact score, at d=3 and with its schedule separated, the sensitivity runs of R3-C10, the grading variants of R3-C15, the all-placement average, drift, development devices) is exploratory and labelled so.

**Better and worse after Holm.** A family comparison is *better after Holm* when Holm's step-down procedure rejects it at α = 0.05 (its adjusted p-value is at most 0.05) and the estimated difference P_L(choice) − P_L(reference) is below zero. It is *worse after Holm* when Holm rejects it and the difference is above zero. Claims use only this z-test with Holm. Per-case tables give the 95% Newcombe interval and its verdict (section 3); the two can disagree at the margin, and the tables then show both.

**Claim rules**, applied per layout and per reference to the family's comparisons. The threshold is *t* = ⌊*n*/2⌋ + 1 for a layout's *n* planned repetition cases: a claim needs a significant win in more than half of them (revision 5 states this derivation; the values are revision 4's). The heavy-hex layout is FakeBoston and FakePittsburgh, with 8 repetition cases and *t* = 5. The square lattice is FakeBerlin alone, with 4 repetition cases and *t* = 3. Each wording names the layout's devices and the noise model: "on FakeBoston and FakePittsburgh (heavy-hex), under this noise model", or "on FakeBerlin (square lattice), under this noise model"; below, *on D* stands for that phrase.

| Wording | Condition |
| --- | --- |
| "beats *X* on D" | better after Holm in at least *t* cases, and worse after Holm in none |
| "mixed against *X* on D: better in *N* cases, worse in *M*" | better after Holm in at least one case and worse after Holm in at least one; every worse case is named |
| "loses to *X* on D" | worse after Holm in at least one case and better after Holm in none; every worse case is named |
| "better after Holm in *N* of *n* cases (fewer than *t* = *T*); no layout claim against *X* on D" | anything else: better after Holm in fewer than *t* cases, and worse in none (revision 6; revision 5 said "no difference shown", which reads as evidence of no difference) |

On FakeBoston and FakePittsburgh every layout claim also gives each device's cases better and worse after Holm, so a reader can see when one device carries it (revision 6). Every claim on FakeBerlin carries the note that its device-level aggregates were seen before F4 (section 1; `claim_rules.device_notes`). For the surface code on FakeBerlin, per reference: "beats" when better after Holm, "loses to" when worse after Holm, otherwise "no difference shown", qualified by R3-R6's headroom check, with the same naming of device and noise model. A case with no valid placement for a method or a reference leaves the family, which shrinks accordingly, and the report names it; *t* stays 5 or 3. The claims are about these snapshots under this noise model; they do not extend to the real chips, to other days' calibrations, or to other devices of the same family (K17, K18). FakeKingston, FakeAachen and FakeNighthawk are reported beside the claim devices and decide no claim.

**Secondary endpoints, pre-registered but not in either family:** cross-fitted relative regret of each method on every held-out case; Spearman's ρ between each score and simulated P_L per held-out case; for every case, the all-placement mean P_L where every placement is simulated, and each method's percentile among the simulated placements (the share with lower pooled P_L), and in R3-C5's best-found cases also among the uniform sample, beside every naive figure (R3-R7); every P_L also per round, 1 − (1 − P_L)<sup>1/rounds</sup>; the drift metrics of R3-D4.

**Model mismatch (revision 5, considered and not added; revision 6 adds R3-C15).** Every family comparison grades a placement under the noise model that chose it. A run that selects under the converted model and grades under a perturbed one with stale decoder weights would add nothing R3-D5 and R3-D1 to R3-D4 do not already measure: R3-D5 is that design with independent log-normal factors per channel at three strengths, and the real-drift runs do it with real calibration change on CZ devices (P2-35). The remaining gap is hardware, which is out of scope (K18). Revision 6 adds the grading variants of R3-C15, which keep each family placement and grade it under a changed model with the selecting model's weights: R3-D5's log-normal design on the CZ devices, and the CZ lengths, idle Z rate, single-qubit layer and readout direction that the converted model fixes. They are exploratory; the family still grades under the model that chose.

## 11. Constraints, scope and prohibited actions

**Constraints** (v1's and v2's still apply; the report discloses these)

| ID | Constraint |
| --- | --- |
| K12 | Reset = readout treats a reset failure as a readout error followed by a wrong flip; real resets may fail differently (leakage, measurement-induced transitions) |
| K13 | FakeNighthawk's calibration is synthetic |
| K14 | Real drift is measured between dated snapshots, each of which carries its own single-calibration noise |
| K15 | Best-found regret in FakeBerlin D7 and FakeNighthawk D5 and D7 (and FakeBerlin D5 if its guard trips, R3-C5) is a lower bound on the true regret. Within v3 this cannot be removed at d=7: simulating every one of 12.8 million placements at the projected 15.6 core-seconds each would take about 56,000 core-hours per setting, against v3's 3,116 in all; FakeBerlin D5 is simulated in full (revision 6), and FakeNighthawk D5 is synthetic and exploratory |
| K16 | mapomatic's cost charges every reset at readout error, so at reset 0 it ranks layouts by reset noise the model does not have (exploratory results only) |
| K17 | Each fake backend is one calibration snapshot, so p-values cover Monte Carlo noise only, not calibration-to-calibration variation; claims are about these snapshots (section 7). Within v3 this cannot be removed: each device ships one snapshot, and the drift runs (Part B) measure what another snapshot does. Revision 6 checked this: across all 84 release tags of `qiskit-ibm-runtime` (git blob hashes only, no contents), FakeBoston, FakePittsburgh, FakeBerlin and FakeMiami each have exactly one props file, unchanged from 0.47.0 to 0.52.0 (`scripts/scan_fake_backend_snapshots.py`, `results_v3/x16/snapshot_scan.json`) |
| K18 | Every confirmatory comparison grades a placement under the model that chose it. Within v3 this cannot be removed: grading under a different, real noise process needs hardware runs, which are out of scope; R3-D1 to R3-D5 and R3-C15 give the closest simulated answers |
| K19 | IBM's published two-qubit error comes from benchmarks whose layers include single-qubit Cliffords, and the circuits charge their H gates again, so single-qubit error around each CZ is partly counted twice; the published error is also measured without neighbouring gates running, so effects of parallel layers (crosstalk) are missing, as in v1's K6. The double count is tested: R3-C15's `cz-without-1q` variant grades every family placement with each CZ error less its two qubits' `sx` errors, the other end of the range. Crosstalk cannot be added within v3: it needs measurements of the devices that the fake backends do not ship (revision 6) |
| K20 | v2's K10 holds for v3: Qiskit is pinned at 2.5.2 by the lock file (2.6.2 is current), a later release may lay out differently, and the Qiskit baselines are 2.5.2's. Within v3 this cannot be changed without breaking the lock and the code freeze every run file is checked against (F4, F5); a later release is a new baseline, and R3-C14 shows how much the choice moves with the transpiler's seed alone (revision 6) |
| K21 | v1's K1 says no exact detector model exists, because Nove's own detector model refuses calibrated two-qubit channels. One does exist for this noise: each CZ channel is 15 equal Pauli terms, which is DEPOLARIZE2 exactly, and each single-qubit Pauli channel factors exactly into independent X, Y and Z flips. Decoding with it instead of Stim's approximate model, which every run uses, changes nothing measurable: on 15 development placements (FakeFez D5 and D7, FakeMiami D5), 1,000,000 shots each, the two decoders disagree on 18 of 15 million shots and their failures are 21,041 against 21,043, while mechanism probabilities differ by at most 4.4% (median 0.14%) (`nac_bench.exact_dem_check`, `results_v3/k1/exact_dem.json`; revision 6) |

**Out of scope for v3:** hardware runs (roadmap Step 7, which needs an IBM Quantum account), ECR devices, surface codes on heavy-hex devices, d=7 surface codes (they need a 13×13 region; the square lattice is 10×12), routing and SWAP insertion, changes to Nove.
