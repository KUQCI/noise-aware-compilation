## 2. Goal and definition of done

v3 asks four questions:

1. **Layout:** does the decoder-graph score still choose better placements than the methods people use, on two chip layouts it was never developed on, with a different native gate (CZ)? (Part A)
2. **Drift:** how much of its gain survives real calibration change, when the decoder also only has the old calibration? (Part B)
3. **Scale:** can a faster version of the score rank surface-code placements, and does its choice beat the naive placement and summed error in simulation? (Part C)
4. **Use:** can someone outside the project see what it does and run it in a few minutes? (Part D)

v3 is done when every test in section 9 passes, the report meets section 10, and every v1 and v2 test still passes. No question has a required answer: a correctly measured loss is an acceptable outcome, and the report gives it the same prominence as a win.

## 4. Part A: new layouts and CZ (roadmap Step 2)

| ID | Requirement |
| --- | --- |
| R3-C1 | The converter accepts CZ devices. A CZ gate error *e* becomes 15 Pauli terms of 1.25 *e* / 15 each, the v1 CX rule; IBM lists `cz` in both directions and each direction is stored as its own channel. H follows v1's rule from the published `sx` error *e*<sub>sx</sub> (three Pauli terms of 1.5 *e*<sub>sx</sub> / 3); `rz` is error-free. Couplers whose two-qubit error exceeds 0.8 are removed, as for CX. A props file with no `reset` entry (FakeMiami) gets a reset duration of the mean readout length plus the mean `x` length, a measurement followed by a conditional flip; on FakeFez this rule gives its published reset length exactly (1,560 + 24 = 1,584 ns), and the conversion notes record it |
| R3-C2 | The repetition codes A (d=3 bit-flip), B (d=3 phase-flip), D5 and D7 (bit-flip), 3 rounds each, are built natively for CZ devices: each CX layer of the v2 circuit becomes three layers, H on the CX targets, CZ on the same pairs, H on the targets. Then, until nothing changes: an H on a qubit and an H on the same qubit in the next layer are both removed (no time passes between them); two consecutive H-only layers on disjoint qubits are merged; empty layers are dropped. An H pair with any other layer between them stays, so the phase-flip code's data idle in the X basis as in v2's circuit B, and B stays a different circuit from A. The bit-flip round becomes H on the ancillas, CZ to each neighbour, H again. The CZ layers keep v2's order and pairs, so a placement uses the same couplers in the same layers; the H layers are their own layers, with idle noise on every waiting qubit |
| R3-C3 | The converter and the CZ circuits are developed and tested on FakeFez, FakeMarrakesh and FakeMiami only. A unit test checks the CZ circuit's noiseless detectors are deterministic and its logical observable is correct |
| R3-C4 | **Selection is exhaustive.** Every method scores every valid placement, at every distance on every device, including FakeNighthawk d=7 (12.8M placements). Each placement is lowered once, by `fast_lowering.Lowering`, which gives the same Stim circuit as Nove's lowering (checked on the development devices as T3-C6 says; every held-out case also checks a seeded sample of 200 placements, or every placement when it has fewer, against Nove's own lowering, and any difference stops the run, leaves the case without a result and is reported), and the v1 score, the decoder-graph score and summed error are computed from it; summed error and mapomatic's layout costs run on every worker. If the timing pilot (R3-C7) projects more than 24 hours on patty for one case, that case's decoder-graph choice comes from the local search of R3-C8 instead, and the report says so |
| R3-C5 | **Evaluation.** On the heavy-hex devices and at d=3 on the square lattice, every valid placement is simulated. At d=5 and d=7 on the square lattice, the simulated set is the union of: every method's choice; the top 30 by each of the decoder-graph score, summed error and mapomatic's cost; and a uniform random sample of 100 valid placements (seeded). Its cross-fitted best is reported as *best found*, with the random sample's size, mean and best-found interval beside it |
| R3-C6 | **Shots.** A pilot stage runs every simulated placement once with 100,000 shots on a separate pilot seed. Each placement's main-stage shots per seed are then the smallest 200,000 × 2^k with 3 × shots × p̂ ≥ *F*, capped at *C*; p̂ = 0 gives the cap. A *family placement* gets *F* = 4,000 (about a 3% relative half-width) and *C* = 102,400,000; every other placement gets *F* = 400 (about 10%) and *C* = 25,600,000. The family placements are, on every case of the confirmatory devices and of FakeNighthawk, at both reset settings: the headline score's choice and the choices of the naive placement, summed error, Qiskit's default at level 2 and mapomatic. FakeKingston's and FakeAachen's placements all get *F* = 400. A placement chosen by several methods is simulated once. At 4,000 failures the smallest relative difference the family's strictest Holm step can call significant is about 7%, and about 4% at its loosest, against about 23% and 14% at 400. Only main-stage counts, on three base seeds, enter any failure rate; pilot counts set shots and may select (R3-C12) but never grade |
| R3-C7 | **Revision 5:** the projection for revision 5's work (two more heavy-hex devices, the idle-aware summed error, the surface code at three *k* values, the family's shots and R3-C10) uses development timings only: patty's second pilot times ratios measured on FakeFez and FakeMiami in one process (`nac_bench.timing_v5`, `results_v3/timing_pilot/projection_v5.json`, P2-38): 1,980 core-hours, 41.3 wall hours on 48 workers, with no case over a limit. A timing pilot on FakeFez d=7 (100 random placements, full selection and main-stage simulation) and on FakeMiami d=7 (enumeration of its 8,493,720 placements and the score on 1,000 random ones, the square-lattice proxy for FakeNighthawk d=7) is run before any held-out conversion; its projection for each held-out case is recorded in `DECISIONS_V3.md`. If a heavy-hex device's exhaustive simulation projects more than 48 hours on patty, that device falls back to R3-C5's best-found set, and the report says so |
| R3-C8 | A fast search (pre-filter and local search, roadmap Step 2.3) is developed on FakeFez, FakeMarrakesh and FakeMiami against their exhaustive scores, frozen at F3, and reported on the held-out devices as exploratory: its choice's score rank and P_L against the exhaustive choice |
| R3-C9 | Qiskit's default and mapomatic read an IBM-shaped Target (P1-5) with `cz` in place of `cx`, and each qubit's reset at its readout error in the reset = readout setting; v2's VF2Layout baseline gets `cz` at its Nove channel total. mapomatic 0.14.0's cost charges every measurement and every reset at the qubit's readout error, whatever the Target's reset error (`layouts.py`), so at reset 0 it sees reset noise the model does not have; only exploratory results are affected (K16) |
| R3-C10 | **Sensitivity runs (revision 5, exploratory).** On every case of the confirmatory devices at reset = readout, each family placement's main-stage samples are decoded a second time with PyMatching's correlated matching (`enable_correlations=True`, PyMatching 2.4.0) built from the same approximate, decomposed DEM; the re-decode reuses the main stage's chunk seeds and must reproduce its uncorrelated counts exactly, so both decoders see the same syndromes. The family placements of D5 and D7 are also simulated in a memory of *d* rounds (5 and 7), with their own pilot (tag `pilot-v3-rounds`), main-stage seeds (`eval-v3-rounds`) and the family's shots rule; A, B and the surface code already have *d* rounds. The family and its claims are recomputed under each run (a case without the run keeps its main-stage counts), and every comparison whose result after Holm changes is listed. No claim uses them |
| R3-C11 | **Idle-aware summed error (revision 5).** For a placement *m* with lowered noisy circuit *C*(*m*) (the circuit selection and simulation use), S<sub>idle</sub>(*m*) = Σ over every noise location ℓ of *C*(*m*) of its total error probability *P*<sub>ℓ</sub>: for a Pauli channel, the sum of its Pauli probabilities (*p*<sub>X</sub> + *p*<sub>Y</sub> + *p*<sub>Z</sub> for a single-qubit channel, the sum of the 15 terms for a two-qubit one), whether it follows a gate or models an idle; for a measurement, its readout flip probability; for a reset, its flip probability. It equals summed error plus the total probability of every idle channel (`baselines.idle_aware_summed_error`, tested on FakeFez and FakeMiami). It sees everything the simulator sees, as one number, with no decoder graph; its choice is the lowest value, ties to the first placement in enumeration order (surface code: then the default schedule, then the mapping). It is scored on every placement and every surface-code candidate |
| R3-C12 | **Pilot-best (revision 5, exploratory).** Among the simulated placements of a case, the one with the fewest pilot failures (pilot shots are equal; ties to the first placement in enumeration order). The pilot runs on its own seed, so pilot-best is graded on main-stage counts like every method, against the headline score and every reference. It is the "just simulate briefly" competitor, at the pilot's 100,000 shots; on the square lattice at d=5 and d=7 it chooses only within R3-C5's simulated set, and the report says so |

## 6. Part C: the scaled score and surface codes (roadmap Steps 4 and 5)

| ID | Requirement |
| --- | --- |
| R3-S1 | The scaled score replaces the exact score's two exponential parts: the distribution of a chain's flipped weight is a dynamic program over edge weights log((1−p)/p) rounded to a grid, and chains are the top *k* by weight from Dijkstra on the doubled graph (detector, observables flipped so far) followed by a k-shortest-paths search. It uses no sampling |
| R3-S2 | *k* and the grid step are chosen on development data only: the smallest *k* (from 1, 2, 5, 10, 20, 50, 100, 200, 500, 1000) for which, on all 10 v2 cases, the scaled score's top choice equals the exact score's and Spearman's ρ between the two over all candidates is at least 0.99. If none does, the *k* with the largest minimum ρ is used, and the report says the criterion failed. **Grid step:** with the chains fixed to the exact score's own minimum-length cycles, the largest step of 0.5, 0.2, 0.1, 0.05, 0.02 and 0.01 for which the scaled score is within 1% of the exact score on every candidate of the 10 cases; it is chosen first, then *k* at that step. **Surface-code *k* (revision 4).** Repetition codes have few near-lightest chains and the surface code has many, so the *k* above can be far too small for it. The held-out runs use the scaled score only on the surface code, with the *k* chosen by convergence on development data: the smallest *k* of 10, 20, 40, 80, 160, 320 and 640 for which, on each of four FakeMiami cases (the d=3 surface code as converted and the d=5 surface code with its removed couplers restored as in R3-S3, each at both reset settings, over every candidate of R3-S4), the scaled score at 2*k* ranks the candidates with Spearman's ρ of at least 0.99 against the score at *k*, and picks the same top choice (selection's rounding, then the default schedule, then the mapping). If none does, the *k* with the largest minimum ρ over the four cases is used (ties to the smaller *k*), and the report says the criterion failed. The grid step stays 0.05, and the repetition-code result above stays recorded. The record also gives each *k*'s Spearman's ρ against R3-S3's simulated P_L; revision 4 called this context that no rule uses, but the *k* chosen after it (below) rests on it, so R3-S3 is the tuning set for the surface-code *k*, not a validation of it. R3-S6 is timed at the chosen *k* and does not constrain it. **Outcome (P2-27):** the criterion failed, because the d=5 ranking never reached ρ ≥ 0.99 between *k* and 2*k*, and the fallback gave *k* = 80. After seeing that result, Khaled chose *k* = 640, the value from the list with the best worst-case ρ against R3-S3's simulated P_L (0.937 at d=5, 0.962 at d=3, against 0.836 and 0.984 at *k* = 80). The choice uses development data only, and the report states it as a deviation from this rule. The ranking it rests on comes from 128 FakeMiami candidates that are 16 qubit sets with 8 placements each, so it has far fewer independent points than candidates (P2-34). **Sensitivity (revision 5):** every surface-code candidate is also scored at *k* = 10 (the repetition-code value) and *k* = 80 (the rule's fallback); their choices are compared with every reference and with the *k* = 640 choice, as exploratory results |
| R3-S3 | **The tuning set (revision 5).** The scaled score is checked against simulated P_L on FakeMiami surface codes (d=3 and d=5) before the freeze; the result is reported, and no threshold is applied. Because the surface-code *k* was chosen on it (R3-S2), the report calls it the tuning set, and the scaled score's only out-of-sample tests are FakeBerlin and FakeNighthawk. FakeMiami has no SWAP-free d=5 embedding (each of the 64 crosses one of its 7 removed couplers), so the d=5 check runs on FakeMiami with those couplers restored at the median CZ channel of the kept ones, labelled synthetic |
| R3-S4 | Surface codes: the rotated surface code, Z-memory, distance 5, 5 rounds, native CZ, on the square lattice. Candidates are every SWAP-free embedding of the patch (shifts, rotations and reflections avoiding removed couplers), each with the standard stabilizer gate orders (N- and Z-shaped for each stabilizer type, the hook-safe combinations only) as schedule candidates. The hook-safe schedules are those with deterministic detectors whose circuit-level distance under uniform noise is *d*: for the Z-memory these are X-type row by row (Z-shaped) with Z-type either column by column (N-shaped, Stim's default) or row by row. An N-shaped X-type order has distance 3 at d=5 |
| R3-S5 | Surface-code baselines: the naive placement with the default schedule; summed error (schedule-blind, so it takes the default schedule); Qiskit's default at level 2 (headline) and levels 1 and 3 (exploratory), and mapomatic (layout only, default schedule) |
| R3-S6 | The score's whole d=5 surface-code selection on one patty core takes at most 60 seconds at the frozen *k*; the time is reported, and a miss is reported as one |
| R3-S7 | The score is presented as a ranking signal, not an error rate, in the CLI output, the API docstrings and the report. No correction factor is fitted |
| R3-S8 | Dropped from the roadmap, with reasons in `v3/EVALUATION.md`: mixed repetition-code schedules (a two-layer schedule must be uniform, so mixing needs a third layer) and the correction factor |

## 7. The headline family and the claim rules

**Family `v3-headline`, 52 comparisons, at reset = readout:**

- 48: the v2 decoder-graph score against the naive placement, lowest summed error, Qiskit's default at level 2 and mapomatic, on circuits A, B, D5 and D7 on FakeBoston, FakePittsburgh and FakeBerlin (revision 5; revision 4 named FakeKingston and FakeAachen).
- 4: the scaled score against the same four references, on the d=5 surface code on FakeBerlin.

FakeNighthawk runs every case, and its 20 comparisons of the same kind are reported beside FakeBerlin's with their Newcombe verdicts and unadjusted p-values, labelled synthetic and exploratory. In the family they could decide no claim and would only make Holm stricter on the real-calibration comparisons (revision 4). FakeKingston and FakeAachen run every case too, and their 32 comparisons are reported the same way, labelled seen and exploratory (revision 5).

**Family `v3-decoder-graph-isolation`, 25 comparisons, at reset = readout (revision 5, secondary, pre-registered).** On the same 13 cases: the decoder-graph score against the idle-aware summed error (R3-C11) and against the v1 score, on circuits A, B, D5 and D7 of FakeBoston, FakePittsburgh and FakeBerlin (24), and the scaled score against the idle-aware summed error on FakeBerlin's surface code (1). Both rivals see the idle noise the summed-error baselines miss, and neither uses a decoder graph, so this family answers whether the decoder graph itself helps. It has its own Holm correction and the same claim rules and wording, reported as "the decoder graph helps beyond …" when the row is *beats*. It decides no headline claim.

**If FakeBerlin has no SWAP-free d=5 surface-code embedding.** FakeMiami has none: each of its 64 d=5 embeddings crosses one of its 7 removed couplers (P2-11). FakeBerlin has 6 removed couplers, and which ones is not read before F4 (section 1). If none of its 64 embeddings avoids them, its 4 surface-code comparisons leave the family, the family has 48 members, and every surface-code result in v3 is exploratory: FakeNighthawk's and the development checks of R3-S2 and R3-S3. v3 then makes no confirmatory surface-code claim, and the report says so (R3-R5). If FakeBerlin has at least one embedding, the 4 comparisons stay in the family however few embeddings there are.

Every other comparison (FakeKingston, FakeAachen, FakeNighthawk, reset 0, Qiskit's default at levels 1 and 3, v2's VF2Layout baseline, the v1 score as a selector, the fast search, pilot-best, the surface code at *k* = 10 and 80, the sensitivity runs of R3-C10, the all-placement average, drift, development devices) is exploratory and labelled so.

**Better and worse after Holm.** A family comparison is *better after Holm* when Holm's step-down procedure rejects it at α = 0.05 (its adjusted p-value is at most 0.05) and the estimated difference P_L(choice) − P_L(reference) is below zero. It is *worse after Holm* when Holm rejects it and the difference is above zero. Claims use only this z-test with Holm. Per-case tables give the 95% Newcombe interval and its verdict (section 3); the two can disagree at the margin, and the tables then show both.

**Claim rules**, applied per layout and per reference to the family's comparisons. The threshold is *t* = ⌊*n*/2⌋ + 1 for a layout's *n* planned repetition cases: a claim needs a significant win in more than half of them (revision 5 states this derivation; the values are revision 4's). The heavy-hex layout is FakeBoston and FakePittsburgh, with 8 repetition cases and *t* = 5. The square lattice is FakeBerlin alone, with 4 repetition cases and *t* = 3. Each wording names the layout's devices and the noise model: "on FakeBoston and FakePittsburgh (heavy-hex), under this noise model", or "on FakeBerlin (square lattice), under this noise model"; below, *on D* stands for that phrase.

| Wording | Condition |
| --- | --- |
| "beats *X* on D" | better after Holm in at least *t* cases, and worse after Holm in none |
| "mixed against *X* on D: better in *N* cases, worse in *M*" | better after Holm in at least one case and worse after Holm in at least one; every worse case is named |
| "loses to *X* on D" | worse after Holm in at least one case and better after Holm in none; every worse case is named |
| "no difference shown against *X* on D" | anything else: better after Holm in fewer than *t* cases, and worse in none |

For the surface code on FakeBerlin, per reference: "beats" when better after Holm, "loses to" when worse after Holm, otherwise "no difference shown", with the same naming of device and noise model. A case with no valid placement for a method or a reference leaves the family, which shrinks accordingly, and the report names it; *t* stays 5 or 3. The claims are about these snapshots under this noise model; they do not extend to the real chips, to other days' calibrations, or to other devices of the same family (K17, K18). FakeKingston, FakeAachen and FakeNighthawk are reported beside the claim devices and decide no claim.

**Secondary endpoints, pre-registered but not in either family:** cross-fitted relative regret of each method on every held-out case; Spearman's ρ between each score and simulated P_L per held-out case; for every case, the all-placement mean P_L where every placement is simulated, and each method's percentile among the simulated placements (the share with lower pooled P_L), beside every naive figure (R3-R7); every P_L also per round, 1 − (1 − P_L)<sup>1/rounds</sup>; the drift metrics of R3-D4.

**Model mismatch (revision 5, considered and not added).** Every family comparison grades a placement under the noise model that chose it. A run that selects under the converted model and grades under a perturbed one with stale decoder weights would add nothing R3-D5 and R3-D1 to R3-D4 do not already measure: R3-D5 is that design with independent log-normal factors per channel at three strengths, and the real-drift runs do it with real calibration change on CZ devices (P2-35). The remaining gap is hardware, which is out of scope (K18).

## 11. Constraints, scope and prohibited actions

**Constraints** (v1's and v2's still apply; the report discloses these)

| K13 | FakeNighthawk's calibration is synthetic |
| K14 | Real drift is measured between dated snapshots, each of which carries its own single-calibration noise |
