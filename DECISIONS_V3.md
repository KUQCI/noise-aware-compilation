# v3 decisions

Choices made while building v3 from the roadmap (*Noise-Aware Placement: Roadmap and GitHub Guide*), with the reason for each. The evaluation of the roadmap itself, including what was dropped and added, is in the project's `v3/EVALUATION.md`. Entries are in the order they were made.

## Phase 1: re-analysis of the v1 and v2 counts (roadmap Steps 6 and 6b)

Everything in this phase was decided after every v1 and v2 result had been seen. It is a labelled re-analysis: it can correct how results were graded, but it is not a pre-registered test. `configs_v3/reanalysis.json` holds every rule and seed; `python -m nac_bench.reanalysis configs_v3/reanalysis.json` writes `results_v3/reanalysis/` without simulating anything.

**P1-1. Verdicts come from the difference interval.** *Better* when the upper end of the 95% Newcombe interval for P_L(choice) − P_L(reference) is below zero, *worse* when its lower end is above zero, otherwise *tie*. Against the all-placement average, the interval of the difference from the average is used, as v1 and v2 printed it. v1's overlap rule ("indistinguishable" whenever the two 95% intervals overlap) is stricter than a 95% test; it contradicted the printed difference interval in 7 v1 comparisons and 3 v2 comparisons, all listed in `REANALYSIS.md`. A comparison of a placement with itself is a tie.

**P1-2. The oracle is cross-fitted.** For each of the three base seeds, the best (and worst) placement is the one with the lowest (highest) pooled P_L on the other two seeds, measured on the held-out seed. The cross-fitted best is the mean over the three folds. A method's regret is the fold mean of P_L(choice) − P_L(fold best) on the held-out seed; its interval is a Wald interval conditional on the picks (each fold's measurement is independent of the data that made its pick), and the relative regret uses the delta method, with the covariance term when the choice is the fold's best. A simulation test (`tests/test_v3_reanalysis.py`) checks the point of the rule: among 80 identical placements, the in-sample best and worst look separated in more than 80% of replications, the cross-fitted ones in fewer than 10%.

**P1-3. Two named Holm families, labelled post hoc.** `v1-headline`: the v1 score against the naive placement on the 11 real-noise base cases of REPORT.md section 1. `v2-headline`: the decoder-graph score against the naive placement, lowest summed error, Qiskit's default transpile at level 1 and mapomatic, on all 10 v2 cases (40 comparisons). The test is the two-sided pooled two-proportion z-test; Holm at α = 0.05. Level 1 stands for Qiskit's default because it is what `transpile` does when no level is given. Levels 2 and 3, v2's VF2Layout baseline and every comparison against the average are exploratory.

**P1-4. Drift reports the absolute gain kept; M6 only when the gain is stable.** For every method, case and drift strength the table gives the original gain P_L(naive) − P_L(true-model choice) and the mean gain kept over draws, both with intervals (the kept gain uses v2's bootstrap: draws resampled, every placement's count redrawn from its binomial). M6 (kept / original) is shown only when the lower end of the original gain's 95% interval is at least half its estimate. This drops M6 where its denominator is near zero; it does not narrow M6 where the numerator is noisy (FakeHanoi d=7 at δ = 0.5 stays 0.17 [−0.45, 0.59]), which is why the absolute value is always shown.

**P1-5. Qiskit's default and mapomatic read the published calibration through an IBM-shaped Target.** `nac_bench.baselines_v3.native_target` builds a Target with `id`, `rz`, `sx`, `x`, the two-qubit gate, `measure` and `reset` from the snapshot's source values: published two-qubit and `sx` gate errors, `rz` error 0, readout errors, no reset error (IBM publishes none), T1 and T2. A test checks it equals the fake backend's own Target on every instruction the conversion kept. v2's `qiskit-vf2` baseline used a different Target (an op-for-op circuit with the Nove channels' total error probabilities); it is kept as it was and labelled "Qiskit VF2Layout (v2 target)".

**P1-6. Qiskit's default transpile runs as shipped.** `generate_preset_pass_manager(optimization_level, target, seed_transpiler=20261021)` at levels 1, 2 and 3, the first v2 base seed. The placement is read from where each circuit qubit's measurements land in the transpiled circuit, not from layout bookkeeping, and it counts as SWAP-free only when the two-qubit gate count is unchanged and every gate sits on a placed interaction edge. A non-SWAP-free result would be reported as such with no counts; none occurred. The pass that produced the final layout is recorded from a pass-manager callback. Ten other seeds are run as context; every level gave one placement across all of them on every case.

**P1-7. mapomatic 0.14.0 runs its README workflow.** Transpile at level 3, `deflate_circuit`, `matching_layouts` on the live coupling map (strict direction), `evaluate_layouts` with `default_cost`, pick the first entry. `SnapshotBackend` serves the native Target through `properties().gate_error`, `properties().readout_error` and `configuration()`, which is all `default_cost` reads; on FakeJakarta, which lost no couplers, a test checks it reproduces mapomatic's costs on the fake backend itself. mapomatic scores a placement and its mirror identically (the same gates on the same qubits), so its pick between the two depends on VF2's enumeration order. Its own pick is the result; the mean over its tied-best set is shown as context.

**P1-8. The fake backends' own Targets are shown as context only.** They still list the couplers the conversion removed (CX error 1.0 in at least one direction), so Qiskit's default can route through one in its working direction: on FakeHanoi it does so at d=5 (coupler 5–8) and d=7 (coupler 19–20) at every level. Those placements are outside the candidate set every method chose from and have no counts. The removal itself is v1's decision D3; restoring the couplers is v1's sensitivity variant.

**P1-9. Machine and versions in every v3 file.** `nac_bench.machine.machine()` records CPU model, logical and usable cores, memory, OS, Python, the C library, the Stim build that loaded and niceness, plus an optional label; the host name is not recorded. `requirements-lock.txt` pins the exact versions behind v1, v2 and v3 (it adds mapomatic 0.14.0 to the environment v1 and v2 ran in) and names the interpreter and the Rust toolchain that built Nove's core here; patty installs from it. Stim 1.16.0's x86-64 wheel contains only its SSE2 and generic builds, so a wider SIMD unit on another machine does not change which code runs; the cross-machine check compares noise-model hashes and counts on two v2 cases, and falls back to agreement of intervals if counts differ.

**P1-10. Step 5.1 mirror check is a pipeline check.** `configs_v3/mirror_check.json` takes the FakeHanoi d=7 mirror pair with the largest separated gap in v2 (2.2e−4 vs 3.3e−4) and simulates each placement with the two CX layers in v2's order and swapped, on three fresh seeds with v2's shot count. Swapping the layers on one placement gives exactly the physical schedule of its mirror in the original order, so matching pairs are physically identical circuits by construction. The check confirms the explanation and the simulation pipeline; it is not new evidence.

## Phase 1b: reset error = readout error (new simulation, pre-registered)

**P1-11. The reset run is pre-registered and sits next to Step 6b.** Every v1 and v2 headline used reset error 0 because IBM publishes none, but IBM resets by measuring and flipping on outcome 1, so reset error ≈ readout error is the more realistic setting. v1's T-L1 variant (v1 score only) turned FakeHanoi d=3 against the naive placement from −12.3% / −2.8% / −15.2% (A1/A3/A5) into +3.4% / +11.4% / +6.0%; under P1-1, A3, A5 and B are worse and A1 a tie, while d=5, d=7 and FakeJakarta keep their gains. v2 never ran it. The run is about the main results, not drift, so it is its own item rather than part of roadmap Step 3. `configs_v3/reset_sensitivity.json` was committed with its seeds and Holm family before any count of the run existed; the devices were already seen at reset 0, which its status says.

**P1-12. What the reset run covers.** At reset = readout: all 10 v2 cases (FakeJakarta is included so the family has the same 40 comparisons as Phase 1's v2-headline), plus A1 and A5 (1 and 5 rounds) on FakeHanoi and FakeAlgiers, also at reset 0, because v2 never ran them and they held two of v1's three flips. v2's A is v1's A3 (identical circuit hash). Every method selects under the model it is graded in (v1's rule): the two scores, summed error and v2's VF2Layout baseline from the Nove model; Qiskit's default and mapomatic from the P1-5 Target with each qubit's reset at its readout error. mapomatic's costs already charge resets at the readout error, but its pick between a placement and its mirror can still change, because its level-3 transpile reads the reset error. Qiskit's default on the Target as IBM publishes it is recorded as context. Fresh base seeds (20261108–10, tag `eval-v3-reset`) and v2's precision rule.

**P1-13. v3's headline setting is reset = readout (Khaled's choice).** v3's pre-registered Holm family uses reset error = readout error; reset 0 runs alongside on every headline device as the bridge to v1 and v2 and is reported, not tested.

## Phase 2: the v3 pre-registration (SPEC_V3.md rev 0)

**P2-1. The v2 score is the headline score for repetition codes; the scaled score for surface codes.** Running v2's frozen decoder-graph score on the new layouts is the direct test of v2's claim. The scaled score is new, exists because the exact score cannot reach surface codes, and gets its own test there. Putting both into the family for repetition codes would double it for two scores that R3-S2 requires to agree.

**P2-2. Selection is exhaustive.** Scoring every placement is affordable even at FakeNighthawk d=7 (about 150 core-hours with the v2 score), so the method's choice never depends on a pre-filter. The fast search the roadmap proposed is still built and reported, against the exhaustive choice, as exploratory.

**P2-3. Two-stage shots.** v1's and v2's rule doubles every placement's shots together until the noisiest estimate is precise, which would give each of FakeKingston's 5,648 d=7 placements the shot count of its best one. A pilot on a separate seed sets each placement's shots for about 400 expected failures; only the main stage's counts are used, so the stopping decision is independent of the counts that are analysed.

**P2-4. One family of 72, claim wording fixed in advance.** 4 held-out devices × 4 repetition circuits × 4 references, plus the d=5 surface code on the two square-lattice devices × 4 references, at reset = readout. Wording per layout and reference: "beats" needs better after Holm in at least 5 of 8 cases and no loss; any loss after Holm is named as a loss; everything else is "no difference shown".

## Phase 2, F2: CZ support on the development devices (SPEC_V3.md rev 1)

**P2-5. CZ devices convert with v1's rules.** `conversion.convert_fake_backend` reads whichever of `cx` and `cz` a props file has (exactly one) and applies the same rules: 15 Pauli terms of 1.25 *e* / 15 per direction, H from the `sx` error, couplers above 0.8 removed (FakeFez 7, FakeMarrakesh 13, FakeMiami 7, all listed at error 1.0). FakeMiami's props have no `reset` entry, so its reset duration is the mean readout length plus the mean `x` length (2,432 ns), a measurement followed by a conditional flip; on FakeFez the same rule gives its published reset length exactly. The generalisation leaves the CX devices' stored snapshots byte for byte as committed (a test reconverts all six devices). The held-out devices' files are not fetched (X16).

**P2-6. Native CZ circuits keep the CX circuit's two-qubit pattern.** `circuits.repetition_code(..., two_qubit_gate="CZ")` replaces each CX layer with H, CZ, H on the CX targets, cancels an H pair only across consecutive layers and merges H-only layers on disjoint qubits. A first version cancelled H pairs across idle windows, which turned the phase-flip circuit B into circuit A (its data would idle in the Z basis); it was replaced before any result used it. The bit-flip round is H on the ancillas, two CZ layers, H; the phase-flip data keep their H gates around each ancilla measurement. Tests check, for circuits A, B, D5 and D7 at 1 and 3 rounds: noiseless determinism, the same two-qubit layers as the CX circuit, and that the shortest undetectable logical error under uniform noise has the code's distance.

**P2-7. The Qiskit baselines read `cz`.** `qiskit_layout.qiskit_circuit` translates CZ to `cz`; `build_target` puts the device's one two-qubit gate (read from its gate durations) at its Nove channel total; `baselines_v3.native_target` already took the gate from the snapshot. On FakeFez, FakeMarrakesh and FakeMiami the native Target equals the fake backend's own Target on every instruction the conversion kept (FakeMiami's own Target has no `reset`). Qiskit's default at level 1 picks the trivial layout (0, 1, 2, 3, 4) on FakeFez d=3, which a later result will grade.

**P2-8. FakeMiami d=7 is the timing proxy for FakeNighthawk d=7.** The d=7 repetition code has 8,493,720 placements on FakeMiami (2.5 minutes to enumerate on one core) against 12.8 million on FakeNighthawk, so the timing pilot (R3-C7) also times FakeMiami d=7. Its d=5 count is 263,404.

## Phase 2, toward F3: the scaled score and the surface code (SPEC_V3.md rev 2)

**P2-9. The scaled score's grid step is 0.05 and k is 10, by R3-S2's fallback.** `nac_bench.scaled_calibration` (`results_v3/scaled/calibration.json`) compares the scaled score with v2's exact score on all 708 candidates of the 10 v2 cases, after checking that the recomputed exact scores equal the recorded ones. Grid step: on the exact score's own cycles the dynamic program stays within 1% at steps 0.05, 0.02 and 0.01, and 0.1 misses by 1.02% on FakeHanoi D5, so the step is 0.05. k: no k gives the exact score's top choice on all 10 cases (at best 7), so the pre-registered criterion fails, and the k with the largest minimum Spearman's rho is used: k = 10 (minimum rho 0.916). The report says the criterion failed. The two scores rank differently because the exact score keeps only minimum-length cycles and the scaled score the most likely chains, which on these devices include longer ones. As context (no rule uses it), against the placements' simulated P_L in the v2 runs the scaled score at k = 10 has mean rho 0.930 and the exact score 0.926; at d=7 larger k ranks better (0.98 at k = 200 on both devices) and at d=3 worse.

**P2-10. Two hook-safe surface-code schedules.** `surface_code.surface_code_stim` with the default schedule reproduces Stim's `rotated_memory_z` two-qubit layers exactly (d=3 and d=5). Of the four N/Z combinations all are valid and deterministic, but only an X-type row-by-row (Z-shaped) order keeps circuit distance 5 at d=5; an N-shaped X-type order has distance 3. So the candidates of R3-S4 are every embedding with (Z, N), Stim's default, and with (Z, Z).

**P2-11. FakeMiami has no d=5 surface code.** On its 10 × 12 lattice the d=5 patch has 64 embeddings (8 shifts × 8 rotations and reflections), and each crosses one of the 7 removed couplers (66–67 and 74–84 sit in the middle); d=3 has 176. R3-S3's d=5 check therefore runs with those couplers restored at the median CZ channel, labelled synthetic. On the held-out side, FakeNighthawk has no removed coupler, so it has all 64 embeddings × 2 schedules; FakeBerlin has 6 and may have none, in which case its four surface-code comparisons leave the family (section 7). Which couplers Berlin lost is a calibration value and is not read before F4.

**P2-12. FakeBoston and FakePittsburgh were read.** Looking for a second square-lattice development device, I read their configuration and properties: both are 156-qubit heavy-hex with CZ, with 2 and 9 couplers above error 0.8. Nothing was converted, scored or simulated. They stay unused, but they are no longer untouched, and SPEC_V3.md section 1 now says so.

**P2-13. Surface-code selection time is close to R3-S6's limit.** Lowering one d=5, 5-round candidate through Nove takes about 0.4 s here and the scaled score with k = 10 about 0.015 s, so 128 candidates take about 55 s on one core of this machine. It is timed on patty before F3.

## Phase 2, before F3: patty

**P2-14. patty reproduces v2 bit for bit (T3-R2).** On patty (AMD EPYC 7401, 96 logical cores, glibc 2.35, Python 3.13.18, Stim's SSE2 build), a clean HTTPS clone of de82171 installed from `requirements-lock.txt` (every pinned version matched; `tzdata` is one unpinned extra) passed all 215 tests, and `run_v2` re-ran v2-hanoi-A (80 placements, 80 drift draws) and v2-algiers-D7 (86 placements, 3.2M shots per seed) under `nice -n 19` with 48 workers. Every per-seed count, every selection and every drift selection equals the committed result (`results_v3/cross_machine/patty.json`). The record's machine block shows niceness 0 because the summarizer, not the simulation, ran without `nice`.

**P2-15. The timing pilot puts the square-lattice d=7 cases over R3-C4's limit.** `results_v3/timing_pilot/timing_pilot.json` (patty, 48 workers, 370 s) projects, for both reset settings:

| Device | Placements (A/B, D5, D7; upper bounds) | Wall hours per setting | Over a limit |
|---|---|---|---|
| FakeKingston | 856, 2,662, 7,790 | 0.85 (0.72 of it simulating D7) | no |
| FakeAachen | 856, 2,662, 7,790 | 0.85 | no |
| FakeNighthawk | 8,312, 345,408, 12,841,728 | 62.0 | D7 selection |
| FakeBerlin | 8,312, 345,408, 12,841,728 | 62.0 | D7 selection |

The total is 251.5 wall hours. Simulation is small everywhere, so R3-C7's 48-hour fallback for FakeKingston and FakeAachen does not apply. The cost is selection on the two square-lattice d=7 cases: 12.8 million placements at about 0.28 s for the decoder-graph score and 0.26 s for the v1 score per placement on a patty core (1,962 core-hours; R3-C4 assumed about 150), plus 19.8 hours of summed error and mapomatic in one process, which is 60.3 wall hours per setting against R3-C4's 24. As pre-registered, those two cases' decoder-graph choice would come from the local search of R3-C8. The time is almost all Nove's lowering and Stim's error model, which both scores repeat for every placement.

**P2-16. R3-S3: the scaled score on FakeMiami surface codes.** `nac_bench.surface_check` (`results_v3/scaled/surface_check.json`) scored and simulated every candidate at reset = readout, 600,000 shots each over three seeds (tag `v3-surface-check`). No threshold applies.

| Case | Candidates | Spearman's rho with P_L: scaled / exact / summed error | P_L of the choice: naive, scaled, exact, summed error, best simulated |
|---|---|---|---|
| FakeMiami d=3 | 352 | 0.976 / 0.985 / 0.898 | 0.0458, 0.0420, 0.0436, 0.0421, 0.0418 |
| FakeMiami d=5, couplers restored (synthetic) | 128 | 0.717 / 0.662 / 0.694 | 0.0709, 0.0695, 0.0703, 0.0840, 0.0695 |

The scaled score's choice is within the best placement's interval at d=3 and is the best simulated placement at d=5; both choices use the (Z, Z) schedule. Two things to carry into the report: v2's exact score runs on the d=5 surface code in 0.06 s a candidate, so P2-1's reason for the scaled score as the surface headline (the exact score cannot reach surface codes) is weaker than written, and the headline stays as pre-registered; and FakeMiami's noise is near the surface code's threshold (median P_L 0.065 over 3 rounds at d=3 and 0.102 over 5 rounds at d=5, about 2% a round at both distances), so the d=5 comparisons are about placement on a code that barely corrects.

**P2-17. Selection lowers each placement once, exactly as Nove does, about 77 times faster.** Nove's durations depend on the gate name alone, so the moments and each circuit qubit's idle time are the same for every placement; only qubit labels and probabilities change (Nove also gives every physical qubit below the highest one used an idle channel, which the placement does not affect). `fast_lowering.Lowering` works the moments out once, gets every probability from the same Nove model methods, caches them by physical qubit, and writes the circuit as Stim text with `repr` floats, which Stim parses back exactly. Its circuit equals `placement.lowered_circuit`'s (Stim circuit equality, exact floats) on sampled placements of every development device, circuit and reset setting and of the surface code on both lattices (`tests/test_v3_fast_lowering.py`); the scores computed from it equal the frozen v1 and v2 scores; `placement.py` and `decoder_graph.py`, frozen by v2, are untouched, so the v1 score's loop is copied as `v1_score_from_dem`. A FakeFez d=7 placement now costs about 15 ms here for the v1 score, the decoder-graph score and summed error together, against about 270 ms, and the whole d=5 surface-code selection 8.5 s against 63 s (R3-S6's limit is 60 s on a patty core). mapomatic's layout costs are computed in chunks on the workers and sorted with its own stable sort, which gives the same list as one call (`tests/test_v3_parallel.py`). The timing pilot now times this path, checks every scored placement against Nove, and times R3-S6; its re-run on patty (`timing_pilot_2.json`) decides R3-C4's fallback.

**P2-18. How the fast search's setting is picked (R3-C8), and what was tried before the rule was written.** `fast_search.FastSearch` scores, with the decoder-graph score, the `prefilter` placements of lowest summed error, then `spread` placements on disjoint qubits (walking down the summed-error order, each one sharing no qubit with those picked before it), then `random` placements drawn uniformly with seed 20261108, and runs a steepest descent from the best `starts` of them. Its moves are the mirror placement, a slide of 1 to `max_shift` qubits either way, swapping an end qubit, and a corner flip; at most 20,000 placements are scored, and the choice follows exhaustive selection's rounding and tie-break. `nac_bench.fast_search_dev` scores every placement of every development case (FakeFez, FakeMarrakesh and FakeMiami; A, B, D5 and D7, 3 rounds, CZ; reset = readout and reset 0; FakeMiami d=7 on patty), checks 20 placements a case against Nove's lowering, and runs the search for all 72 settings of `prefilter` in {10, 100}, `spread` in {0, 40}, `random` in {0, 300, 1000}, `starts` in {10, 30} and `max_shift` in {2, 4, 6}. The setting frozen at F3 is the one that finds the exhaustive choice in every development case with the fewest placements scored in the worst case. If none finds it everywhere, it is the one with the smallest worst-case ratio of its choice's score to the exhaustive choice's (both rounded as in selection), then the fewest placements scored in the worst case. Remaining ties go to the smaller `prefilter`, then `spread`, `random`, `starts` and `max_shift`. The development records are merged into `results_v3/fast_search/pick.json` (`--pick-from`).

The first draft of this rule had only the pre-filter and the descent, with slides of 1 or 2. Trial runs on the development cases, not kept as results, showed that summed error points at the wrong part of FakeFez: by summed error the decoder-graph choice there ranks 1,157th to 1,366th of 1,926 placements at d=5 and 1,426th to 1,552nd of 4,614 at d=7 (at d=5 with reset = readout its summed error is 0.83 against 0.30 for the lowest), because summed error counts readout and reset errors in full and the decoder-graph score barely weighs them. None of 36 such settings found the exhaustive choice on FakeFez d=5 or d=7 at either reset setting, while nearly all found it on FakeMarrakesh and FakeMiami. The spread and random starts and the longer slides were added for that reason. With random starts, 10 of 24 probe settings found FakeFez's choice in all six of its A, D5 and D7 cases, for example 300 random placements and 10 descents with slides up to 4, scoring at most 502 placements. Only development devices were used, and only the development grid changed.

**P2-19. The second timing pilot: every held-out case fits, so selection is exhaustive everywhere (R3-C4, R3-C7, R3-S6).** On patty at 46b2d4d (`results_v3/timing_pilot/timing_pilot_2.json`, 389 s, peak 2.0 GB in one process), the prepared lowering matched Nove's on all 1,340 scored placements, and the tests of the new lowering, the parallel mapomatic, the timing projection and v2's results passed there (39 tests). A FakeMiami d=7 placement costs 31 ms for all three scores (21 ms of it the decoder-graph score), against 540 ms in the first pilot. The projection for both reset settings:

| Device | Selection, wall hours per setting | Simulation, wall hours per setting | Both settings |
|---|---|---|---|
| FakeKingston, FakeAachen | 0.01 | 0.77 | 1.55 each |
| FakeNighthawk, FakeBerlin | 7.9 (7.7 of it d=7) | 0.22 | 16.2 each |

The total is 35.6 wall hours. No case is over R3-C4's 24 hours or R3-C7's 48, so no fallback applies: every method scores every placement on every held-out case, and the fast search (R3-C8) is only reported, as exploratory. The square-lattice d=7 figure is conservative, as mapomatic's whole time on FakeFez d=7 (one chunk there) is scaled by the layout count; it is 5.3 of the 7.7 hours. The d=5 surface-code selection took 15.3 s on one patty core against R3-S6's 60 s (on FakeMiami with restored couplers, the proxy for FakeNighthawk). Python 3.13 warns that `fork()` in a multi-threaded process may deadlock (in v2's rerun test); no run has hung, and the start method is left as it is, because it does not change any count.
